A programmable reward layer for digital loot across apps and games.

Loot turns one-off coupons and points into interoperable, on-chain rewards that brands, creators, and platforms can issue, track, and redeem without building their own web3 stack.

Ask: pre-seed design & build time; AWS Activate Founders credits. Instrument: simple agreement for future equity (SAFE) or studio-style build partnership.

Why Loot exists

Reward sprawl without a shared rail.

Consumers juggle app-specific points and one-off promo codes. Brands and indie builders lack the time and trust surface to wire up web3-native rewards safely.

  • 1

    Isolated loyalty islands

    Each product runs its own points or promo codes. Nothing moves across brands, games, or tools, so perceived value decays quickly.

  • 2

    Heavy web3 integration tax

    Wallet UX, custody choices, fraud controls, and on-chain state are all table stakes, but out of scope for most small teams.

  • 3

    Regulatory and risk anxiety

    Teams worry about compliance, money transmission, tax exposure, and chain risk even when issuing simple non-cash perks.

Opportunity
A clean reward API for digital loot.

Loot abstracts reward issuance, ownership, and redemption into one opinionated rail that apps and games call over HTTPS, without managing keys or ledgers directly.

Product sketch

A simple programmable reward rail.

For consumers, Loot feels like a unified vault of items and perks. For builders, it is an AWS-hosted API that issues, tracks, and settles digital rewards with clear cost envelopes.

  • ✓

    One vault, many worlds

    Rewards from games, SaaS tools, and brands land in one Loot vault tied to your identity, not a single app login.

  • ✨

    Loot that travels

    Items can unlock perks in partner apps or be burned for other rewards instead of expiring silently.

  • A

    Issue

    POST to Loot with reward type, metadata, and optional on-chain flag. Loot handles ID generation, storage, and optional mint.

  • B

    Own

    Rewards attach to user identities managed via Cognito plus optional wallet. Ownership is queryable and auditable.

  • C

    Redeem

    On redemption, Loot validates rules, marks state, and emits events for in-app fulfillment and accounting.

Business model
Usage-based API with clear floors.

Charge per issued reward plus tiered platform fees once volume and partner count justify it. Keep infra anchored in AWS free and low tiers early on.

Adoption risk

Builders might stay with in-house points.

Mitigation: Ship SDKs, examples, and no-regrets analytics so Loot is faster than rolling a custom system.

Regulatory drift

Loyalty and web3 rules may tighten.

Mitigation: Start with non-cash perks, work with counsel, and keep chain-facing pieces modular.

Platform dependency

Heavy AWS reliance.

Mitigation: Lean into AWS-native services early, then abstract compute where it makes sense once product fit is clearer.

AWS-native rationale

Designed to be a clean fit for AWS Activate.

Loot is intentionally small, serverless, and aligned with AWS Activate Founders: new, pre-Series B startup with a real product, company website, and AWS account in good standing.

Reference architecture
Serverless, low-ops, AWS-first.

Core stack: Amazon API Gateway, AWS Lambda, Amazon DynamoDB, Amazon Cognito, and optionally Amazon Managed Blockchain or partner wallet infra for on-chain state.

Serverless Event-driven Multi-tenant

How this lines up with Activate

AWS Activate Founders targets early-stage, self-funded startups with a functional website, AWS account, and product vision. Loot fits that profile and plans to use credits almost entirely on core services, not experimentation.

  • Meets age and stage guidance for pre-Series B, sub-10-year companies.
  • Designed to keep infra within Activate Founders credit envelopes while validating product-market fit.
  • Clear story for AWS: more apps using more AWS services because Loot lowers the reward-rail barrier.
Credits usage plan
Where AWS credits go.

Primary spend is on Lambda, API Gateway, DynamoDB, Cognito, CloudWatch, and basic observability. No GPU or heavy compute until demand is proven.
